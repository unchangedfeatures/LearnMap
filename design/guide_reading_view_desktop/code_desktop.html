Chapter 04: Probability Distributions

Understanding the Normal Distribution

The normal distribution, often called the Gaussian distribution, is the most important probability distribution in statistics. Many real-world phenomena, from heights and blood pressure to measurement errors, naturally follow this symmetric bell-shaped pattern.

The Empirical Rule

For any normally distributed dataset, the following approximations hold true:

  • 68% of data falls within one standard deviation (σ) of the mean (μ).
  • 95% falls within two standard deviations.
  • 99.7% falls within three standard deviations.
lightbulb

lightbulb Real-world Example

Imagine a factory producing lightbulbs. If the average lifespan is 1,000 hours with a standard deviation of 50 hours, the normal distribution tells us that roughly 95% of all bulbs will last between 900 and 1,100 hours. This predictability allows companies to set warranty periods accurately.

key Key Takeaway

Central Limit Theorem ensures that as sample sizes grow, the distribution of the sample mean will approach a normal distribution, regardless of the population's shape. This is why normal distribution is the bedrock of inferential statistics.
